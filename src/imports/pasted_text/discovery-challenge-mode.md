# Add a Discovery Challenge Mode

Add a new type of challenge to the existing product.

This should be one of the most interesting parts of the experience.

The goal is to expose users to **ideas, concepts, phenomena, stories, mental models, psychological effects, historical events, strange facts, business concepts, philosophical ideas, and cultural phenomena that they may not already know about**.

The product should not only ask users:

> “What do you think about this?”

Instead, it should sometimes introduce them to something unfamiliar and challenge them to **understand it, think about it, form an opinion, and create something from it**.

---

# The Discovery Flow

The experience should follow:

**Discover → Get a hint → Think → Create**

The user clicks:

### 🎲 Discover Something

Then the existing slot-machine animation begins.

Instead of only showing generic writing prompts, the animated reel cycles through interesting concepts and ideas.

Examples passing through the reel:

* The Cobra Effect
* Dunbar's Number
* The Dunning–Kruger Effect
* Survivorship Bias
* The IKEA Effect
* The Ship of Theseus
* Parkinson's Law
* The Streisand Effect
* The Baader–Meinhof Phenomenon
* The Hedonic Treadmill
* Goodhart's Law
* The Overton Window
* The Broken Windows Theory
* The Tragedy of the Commons
* The Peter Principle
* The Butterfly Effect

Also mix in:

* Strange historical events
* Unexpected scientific discoveries
* Psychological experiments
* Internet culture phenomena
* Business failures
* Famous mistakes
* Counterintuitive facts
* Philosophy
* Behavioral economics
* Social phenomena

The user should feel:

> “Wait, what is that?”

---

# The Hint Reveal

When the slot machine lands, do NOT immediately give the user a full explanation.

First reveal only the name.

For example:

# The Cobra Effect

Then underneath:

### A hint

> Sometimes, trying to solve a problem makes the problem worse.

Pause the experience slightly.

Let the user think.

Then provide a button:

**Tell me more →**

When clicked, reveal a very short explanation:

> The Cobra Effect happens when an incentive designed to solve a problem unintentionally creates worse outcomes.

Keep the explanation concise.

Maximum 2–4 sentences.

Do not turn this into a learning platform or Wikipedia page.

The purpose is to give the user **just enough context to start thinking**.

---

# Then Give Them a Challenge

After understanding the concept, the user chooses what they want to do with it.

Show:

# What will you create?

### ✍️ Write

Create a LinkedIn post, newsletter, opinion, or story inspired by this idea.

### 🎤 Speak

Explain the idea or share your opinion about it.

### 🧠 Think

Connect the idea to a real-world situation.

# Challenge Categories

When generating discovery topics, randomly select from categories such as:

### 🧠 Psychology

Cognitive biases, human behavior, psychological phenomena.

### 💡 Mental Models

Interesting frameworks for thinking and decision-making.

### 📚 History

Strange events, mistakes, inventions, and moments that changed the world.

### 🔬 Science

Counterintuitive discoveries and surprising phenomena.

### 💼 Business

Failures, strategies, incentives, and unexpected business lessons.

### 🌍 Society

Cultural phenomena and social behavior.

### 🤯 Weird

Things that sound unbelievable but are real.

The category should be shown subtly before or after the reveal.

Do not make users manually browse a huge database.

Discovery should feel spontaneous.

---

# Writing Challenge After Discovery

Once the user receives an idea and short explanation, generate a challenge based specifically on that concept.

The challenge should not always be:

> “Explain this concept.”

Instead, make the user think.

Examples:

### Connect

> Where have you seen this happen in real life?

### Apply

> How would this concept apply to modern startups?

### Challenge

> Do you agree with this idea? Why or why not?

### Story

> Tell a personal story that demonstrates this concept.

### Create

> Write a LinkedIn post explaining this idea without using its official name.

### Contrarian

> Argue against this idea.

### Explain

> Explain this concept to a 10-year-old.

This ensures that the user is **creating something**, not simply summarizing information.

---

# Add an Optional Hint System

Before starting the challenge, provide a small:

### Need a hint?

button.

Do not immediately reveal the answer.

Hints should appear progressively.

### Hint 1

A simple question that guides thinking.

Example:

> Can you think of a situation where solving one problem accidentally created another?

### Hint 2

A real-world example.

Example:

> Think about social media algorithms designed to increase engagement.

The user can choose whether to use hints.

This creates a feeling similar to solving a puzzle.

# Important Product Philosophy

This feature should create the following loop:

> **I didn't know this existed.**

↓

> **Oh, that's interesting.**

↓

> **Where have I seen this before?**

↓

> **What do I think about it?**

↓

> **Let me create something from it.**

The goal is not just communication practice.

The product should help people build the habit of being **curious, thinking deeply, connecting ideas, and expressing themselves**.

---

# Keep the Experience Minimal

Do NOT turn this into:

* A Wikipedia clone
* An online course
* A feed of educational content
* An endless scrolling discovery app
* A social network

The user gets **one interesting thing at a time**.

They learn just enough to become curious.

Then they must do something with it.

The core loop remains:

# Discover → Think → Create

Make this feel like the user has stumbled upon an idea they want to immediately explore.

The product should create anticipation not only around:

> “What challenge will I get?”

But also:

> **“What am I about to discover?”**
